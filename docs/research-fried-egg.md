# Research: Perfect Fried Egg (Sunny-Side Up & Over-Easy)

Chef-quality technique notes for Glutt “Cooking basics” / Polly voice coaching.  
Compiled from primary culinary-technique and food-science sources (Serious Eats, America’s Test Kitchen, Culinary Institute of America, American Egg Board / Eggs.ca, USDA/FDA, RSC kitchen chemistry). Where sources disagree, disagreements are noted and a **beginner-safe recommendation** is marked.

## 1. Technique overview (science in plain English)

A fried egg is a race between two proteins that set at slightly different temperatures.

| Part | Starts to set | Firmly set | Source |
|------|---------------|------------|--------|
| Egg white (albumen) | ~140°F / 60°C (jelly-like) | ~149–158°F / 65–70°C | Eggs.ca; Kansas State food science (LibreTexts) |
| Egg yolk | ~144°F / 62°C (AEB: 149°F / 65°C) | ~158°F / 70°C | Eggs.ca; American Egg Board |

Sources differ slightly on the yolk’s *start* point (Eggs.ca ~144°F; AEB ~149°F), but they agree on the useful cooking fact: **whites firm before yolks fully set**. That is why you can have opaque whites and a still-runny yolk.

**Why whites go rubbery:** Overheating tightens the protein network and squeezes out water (syneresis). Eggs.ca: overheated proteins “tighten and squeeze out the moisture, leaving the eggs dry, firm and rubbery.” Gentle heat (or steam from above) sets the white without forcing that tight, dry matrix.

**Why eggs stick:** Protein-rich foods bond to metal. The Royal Society of Chemistry (Kitchen Chemistry): proteins form complexes with metal atoms (e.g. iron) in the pan. Nonstick coatings block that chemistry; fat fills microscopic valleys and creates a barrier. Beginners should not fight stainless steel for their first fried eggs.

**The top-white problem:** Heat comes mainly from the pan bottom. The thin film of white *on top of the yolk* is the last to turn opaque—the classic “jelly-clear” or “snotty” patch. Pros solve it without wrecking the yolk by:
1. **Lid / steam** (ATK hybrid fry-steam; CIA water-drop + lid; Kenji “grandma” splash of water + lid)
2. **Basting** with hot fat (CIA; Kenji crispy oil basting; Marco Pierre White butter basting)
3. **Flipping** briefly (over-easy)

**Food safety note (do not bury this):** USDA/FDA advise cooking eggs until yolks are firm for safety; American Egg Board notes sunny-side / over-easy may not be suitable for high-risk people. Coaching below targets the *culinary* runny-yolk style adults commonly want. Mention pasteurized shell eggs for immunocompromised users.

## 2. Equipment & ingredients checklist

### Equipment
- **8–10 inch nonstick skillet** for 1–2 eggs (beginner default). ATK uses 12–14″ nonstick for 4 eggs (or 8–9″ with half the fat for 2). CIA: “nonstick surfaces are useful.”
- **Lid that fits** (glass lid ideal—you can watch without losing steam). ATK and Serious Eats both use covering.
- **Thin flexible spatula** (fish spatula is excellent for sliding eggs off—ATK tip).
- **Small bowl or cup** to crack into first (CIA, ATK).
- Spoon (for basting, optional).

### Ingredients (1–2 eggs, beginner path)
- 1–2 **fresh** large eggs (USDA Grade AA/A: thick whites hold shape; best for frying/poaching—USDA FSIS).
- Fat — pick one approach:
  - **Beginner classic:** ~1 Tbsp unsalted butter for 2 eggs (Serious Eats sunny-side / over-easy).
  - **ATK diner hybrid:** 2 tsp vegetable oil + 2 tsp chilled unsalted butter for 4 eggs (halve for 2).
  - American Egg Board foodservice guide: about **1 teaspoon fat per egg** to avoid toughening from too little fat / greasiness from too much.
- Kosher salt, black pepper; optional chili flakes or finishing butter.

### Pan choice (covered in depth)

| Pan | Pros | Cons | Beginner? |
|-----|------|------|-----------|
| **Nonstick** | Releases easily; forgiving heat; CIA/ATK preferred path | Soft spatula only; coating wears | **Yes — default** |
| **Well-seasoned cast iron / carbon steel** | Great browning; Kenji prefers for crispy basted eggs | Needs good seasoning + enough fat; stick risk | Intermediate |
| **Stainless** | Durable | Proteins bond to metal (RSC); needs Leidenfrost-level preheat + fat skill | Advanced — skip for first-timers |

**Size:** Don’t pinball one egg in a huge empty skillet (edges race ahead). Match pan to egg count: ~8″ for one, 8–10″ for two, larger only when cooking more (ATK scaling).

## 3. Sunny-side-up: step-by-step chef script

*Spoken coaching voice. Beginner-safe path = gentle butter + lid (Serious Eats classic sunny-side + CIA moderate heat + ATK “look at the white around the yolk” cue). Times are approximate—stoves vary; trust eyes/ears.*

---

**Before you start**

“Okay. We’re making a sunny-side-up egg: set white, runny yolk, no flip. Grab a nonstick pan that fits your egg—don’t use a giant skillet for one lonely egg. Crack the egg into a small bowl first. If the yolk broke in the bowl, scramble it later; don’t fight a broken yolk in the pan. Fresh eggs help a lot—thick white that sits up tall instead of flooding the pan.”

“Fridge-cold is fine. If you have fifteen minutes, sitting the egg out takes a little chill off so it cooks more evenly—but don’t leave eggs out for hours. Food-safety folks want them cold until you cook.”

---

**Fat & heat**

“Put the pan on **medium-low to medium**—what restaurant schools call moderate heat. Too cold and the egg can glue itself down; too hot and the edges blister and brown while the top is still jelly. We’re aiming for patient, not panicked.”

“Add about a tablespoon of butter for two eggs—or a teaspoon or so per egg. Watch the butter: it should melt, then foam. When the foam **subsides** and the butter looks glossy—not smoking, not browning into nutty brown for this gentle style—you’re ready. If it smells toasted-brown already, the pan’s too hot; pull it off a second and turn the heat down.”

*(Disagreement note: Marco Pierre White wants almost silent, ultra-low butter with zero sizzle. America’s Test Kitchen goes hotter—oil shimmering at medium-high—then lids and finishes off-heat. Kenji’s crispy street style is medium-high oil with aggressive basting. For a nervous first-timer wanting tender diner-style eggs, **medium / medium-low + lid** is the safest middle.)*

---

**Adding the egg**

“Hold the bowl right down by the pan—almost kissing the butter—and tip the egg in gently. Don’t drop it from high up; you’ll break the yolk or splash hot fat. If you want the yolk dead-center, you can tip the pan a little so the white pools around it. Then leave it alone. No poking.”

---

**What the white should look like (stages)**

“Right away the edges turn from clear to cloudy. That’s the white starting to set—around that 140°F zone. You’ll hear a soft gentle sizzle, not a violent spit-and-scream. If it’s roaring and the edges are already brown and cratered, heat’s too high.”

“After about a minute, the outer white should look **opaque**—solid white, not see-through. The middle, especially that thin veil on top of the yolk, often still looks shiny and translucent. That’s normal. That’s the part we’re going to steam.”

“Cover with a lid now. Steam is your friend for sunny-side. You’re cooking from above without flipping. Peek by cracking the lid just a little so you don’t dump all the steam—ATK’s move.”

*(Optional steam boost—CIA / Kenji grandma style: “If the top’s stubborn, splash a teaspoon of water into the pan—not on the yolk—and clap the lid back on. You’ll hear a quick hiss. Steam sets that jelly patch.”)*

*(Optional basting instead of lid: “Or tip the pan, pool the hot butter, and spoon it over the white only—avoid the yolk unless you want it more set.”)*

---

**Yolk management**

“Don’t poke the yolk. Don’t press it. You’re done for classic sunny-side when: (1) the white is fully opaque including that ring around the yolk, (2) the yolk is still glossy and jiggles if you nudge the pan, and (3) the underside isn’t burnt. CIA’s definition: white cooked through; yolk bright yellow, soft and runny.”

“Serious Eats’ classic timing is roughly **a minute and a half covered, then a minute and a half uncovered**—about three minutes total—but your stove might be faster. ATK’s hotter method is only about a minute under the lid, then **off the burner**, still covered, for fifteen to forty-five seconds for a runny yolk. Either way: **look at the white around the yolk**. When that thin layer goes from jelly-clear to opaque, you’re there.”

---

**Seasoning & plate**

“Salt at the end, or very lightly once the white has started to set. Salt on raw white can pull little beads of moisture and make the surface look spotty—Kenji’s frying tip is season at the finish for that reason. A pinch of salt, a crack of pepper, maybe chili flakes. Slide the egg out with a thin spatula—don’t flip it in the air. Hot plate if you have one. Eat now; eggs wait for no one.”

---

**Full spoken sequence (tight version for Polly)**

1. “Crack into a bowl. Nonstick pan, medium-low. Butter in—wait for foam to settle, fat shimmering, no smoke.”
2. “Tip the egg in close to the pan. Soft sizzle. Don’t touch it.”
3. “Watch the white: clear → cloudy → opaque from the edges in.”
4. “When the edges look set but the top by the yolk is still shiny-clear, lid on. Optional: tiny splash of water, lid back.”
5. “About one to three minutes depending on heat. Peek. White around the yolk should be opaque, not jelly. Yolk still glossy and wobbly.”
6. “Salt and pepper now. Slide onto the plate. You did it.”

## 4. Over-easy variant script (differences only)

*Same start as sunny-side through the first side. Then:*

“Over-easy is insurance. Same butter, same moderate heat, same crack-into-a-bowl. Cook until the whites are **nearly set on top** and the yolk still looks wet—Serious Eats says about **two minutes** on the first side. You should still see a little unset sheen near the yolk; if you wait until the first side is bone-dry, the flip will overcook the yolk.”

“Spatula under the egg—get the whole white supported. Commit. Flip with confidence in one motion; hesitant half-flips break yolks. Cook the second side only **five to ten seconds**—CIA/Serious Eats over-easy: white cooked through, yolk still runny. Longer than that and you’re drifting into over-medium.”

“Season after the flip or on the plate. Slide off. That’s over-easy: fully cooked white, no translucent bits, yolk that still runs when you cut.”

**Doneness ladder (CIA):**
- Over easy → yolk still runny  
- Over medium → yolk slightly thickened, still flowing  
- Over hard → yolk completely set

## 5. Doneness visual / auditory guide

Use these instead of a kitchen timer alone.

### See
- **Raw / just in:** White translucent and spreading; yolk tall and glossy (fresher = taller).
- **Early set:** Edges opaque; center still clear/shiny.
- **Ready sunny-side:** Entire white opaque, including the thin film hugging the yolk; yolk still bright, convex, glossy; optional light fringe browning OK if you like diner edges—uniform dark brown rubber edge = too hot/too long.
- **Ready over-easy (pre-flip):** Whites nearly set on top; yolk still liquid-looking.
- **Overcooked white:** Shriveled, deeply browned, tough, weeping watery liquid.
- **Overcooked yolk:** Center dull, matte, no jiggle; cuts like soft-boiled firm yolk.

### Hear
- **Gentle:** Soft continuous sizzle = good moderate heat.
- **Too hot:** Loud sputtering, aggressive crackling, butter smoking.
- **Too cold / wet:** Almost silent, egg sits pale and may stick when you try to move it (CIA: heat too low → stick risk).
- **Steam lid:** Soft hiss when water hits fat; quiet steam under lid.

### Feel (spatula)
- Egg releases and slides when underside is set; if it tears, wait 10–20 more seconds (protein needs to finish coagulating before it lets go).

### ATK off-heat cues (lid method)
- Runny yolks: 15–45 sec off heat — “white around edge of yolk will be barely opaque”
- Soft-set yolks: 45–60 sec  
- Medium-set yolks: ~2 minutes

## 6. Failure recovery tips

| Failure | Why it happened | Fix now / next time |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Stuck egg** | Bare metal contact; heat too low too long; not enough fat; stainless without skill | Don’t scrape violently. Add a dab of butter at the edge, wait for release. Next time: nonstick + enough fat + moderate heat (CIA). |
| **Broken yolk** | Aggressive crack, drop from height, poking, timid flip | Crack into bowl; pour low; for over-easy flip decisively with full spatula support. Broken → make it “over scramble” or basted broken-yolk egg; still tasty. |
| **Browned rubbery white** | Heat too high; cooked too long | Lower heat; pull earlier; prefer lid/steam over prolonged hard fry. Slight brown lace can be delicious (Kenji crispy)—uniform tough brown is not. |
| **Undercooked white, set/overdone yolk** | Bottom heat only; no steam/baste/flip; heat too high under yolk | Lid + steam, baste whites only, or brief over-easy flip. Or ATK: finish **off heat** covered so residual steam sets white without cooking yolk harder. |
| **Greasy egg** | Too much fat; fat not hot enough so egg poaches in oil | Use ~1 tsp fat per egg (AEB); fat should shimmer/foam-subside before egg goes in. Drain on plate briefly if needed. |
| **Watery / spotty white** | Very fresh watery outer white *or* salt on raw white drawing beads | Blot or trim ultra-thin outer white if needed; salt late (Kenji frying tip). Older eggs spread more—use fresher for frying (USDA Grade AA). |
| **Butter burned** | Pan too hot before egg | Start cooler; ATK trick: oil in pan first (higher smoke point), butter only at the last second. |
| **Egg steamed soggy, no flavor** | Too much water under lid | Use a **splash** (teaspoon), not a puddle; or baste with butter/oil instead. |

## 7. Method map (when to use which)

| Goal | Method | Heat | Fat | Key move |
|------|--------|------|-----|----------|
| Tender classic sunny-side (beginner) | Lid steam | Medium / med-low | Butter | Cover; watch white around yolk |
| Diner frizzled edge + runny yolk | ATK hybrid | Oil low-preheat → med-high shimmer | Oil + butter | 1 min covered → off heat covered |
| Crispy lacy “really fried” | Kenji basting | Medium-high | 2–3 Tbsp oil | Spoon hot oil over whites ~45–60 sec |
| French/ultra-gentle (Marco Pierre White) | Butter baste | Very low (almost no sizzle) | Butter | Spoon butter; no browning |
| Guaranteed set white | Over-easy | Medium | Butter | Flip 5–10 seconds |

**Beginner-safe pick for the app:** Tender classic sunny-side with **nonstick + butter + lid** (Serious Eats + CIA), with optional teaspoon of water. Teach over-easy as the “insurance flip.” Mention ATK hybrid and Kenji crispy as level-ups.

## 8. Coaching language bank (sensory phrases)

Reusable lines for Polly / UI:

- “Foam subsides—butter looks calm and shiny, not smoking.”
- “Pour from right above the pan—like you’re sliding it into bed, not skydiving.”
- “Soft sizzle. If it sounds angry, turn the heat down.”
- “White goes from glass-clear to cloudy to opaque—like a fog rolling in from the edges.”
- “That shiny jelly ring around the yolk? That’s the last bit. Lid on—we’ll steam it.”
- “Opaque, not jelly-clear. Yolk still glossy and wobbly. That’s sunny-side.”
- “Spatula under the whole egg—support the white—now flip like you mean it. Five to ten seconds. Off.”
- “Salt at the end so we don’t make the white weep.”
