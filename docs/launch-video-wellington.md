# Glutt Launch Video — Beef Wellington

**Runtime:** 60s · **Tone:** scrappy founder, real kitchen, real mistakes · **CTA:** App Store

---

## The concept in one line

**Two guys who can't cook attempt a beef Wellington — the dish most likely to humiliate you — and Polly catches the mistake that would have ruined it.**

Why this works:

- **Beef Wellington is the highest-stakes dish in home cooking.** Gordon Ramsay's signature, famous for going wrong, and everyone watching knows it. Attempting it is inherently dramatic. You don't have to manufacture tension.
- **The failure modes are visual.** Wet duxelles, gray band, soggy bottom. You can literally see the mistake on camera, which means Polly catching it is *shown*, not claimed.
- **The payoff is a single frame.** The slice. Perfect pink center, crisp pastry. That one shot is your thumbnail, your cold open, and your ending.
- **It earns the vision.** Eliam's whole point in that session: don't shrink the product to what you can build today. "Maybe to have Gordon Ramsay in your house, you're gonna have to have Meta glasses." A Wellington is exactly the dish where you'd want Gordon Ramsay in your house.

**The line the whole video hangs on:** *Recipes tell you what to do. Nobody tells you when you're doing it wrong.*

---

## The three things you're selling (and nothing else)

1. **She hears you.** Hands in raw meat, phone untouched, you say her name and she answers.
2. **She sees you.** She catches the wet duxelles before it ruins the pastry. **This is the aha. Give it the most screen time.**
3. **Any recipe becomes a live cook.** Instagram reel → share sheet → cookable recipe. One fast beat, 4 seconds.

**Do not show:** pantry, meal plan, grocery list, macros, Progress tab, onboarding, the paywall. All good features. All death in a launch video. Two or three magic moments, nothing else.

## The script

Word counts are the point. Conversational delivery runs about **2.5 words per second**. Don't add words on the day, you will blow the runtime.

### 0:00–0:05 — COLD OPEN

> **Shot:** Extreme close-up, knife entering the Wellington. Blade pulls through slowly, crust shatters, the slice falls open. Hold two seconds on the pink center.
>
> **Audio:** Crust crackle only. Room tone. No music, no voice.
>
> **Text:** none

Silence for five seconds is uncomfortable, which is exactly why it holds people. Do not put music here.

### 0:05–0:07 — SMASH CUT

> **Shot:** Hard cut. Same kitchen, three hours earlier. Raw Wellington half-wrapped, duxelles everywhere, Malik's hands covered, Omar mid-frame doing something unhelpful.
>
> **Text (slams in, no animation, holds 1.5s):**
> **NEITHER OF US COULD COOK.**
>
> **Audio:** Music enters on the cut, single hit.

### 0:07–0:18 — BACKSTORY

**Malik, to camera. Apron, flour on him, kitchen alive behind.**

> "I moved to Miami three months ago and I ate out every night. It's not that I can't follow a recipe. It's that I never know if I'm doing it right."

*(27 words ≈ 11s)*

**Why this line:** it's the real thing you said in the session with Eliam, almost verbatim. Don't polish it into a commercial. The unpolished version is the asset.

### 0:18–0:28 — THE ONE-LINER

**Omar, to camera, phone in hand with Polly's screen visible.**

> "So we built Glutt. It teaches you to cook like a world-class chef. Polly talks you through every step, while watching you do it."

*(24 words ≈ 9s)*

This is the two-liner you already workshopped, and it survives contact. Keep "watching." You lost that argument in the room for a good reason: watching is the product. Saying "showing" hides the actual bet.

**Delivery note:** don't blink on "watching you do it." Say it flat and certain, like it's obvious. Commercial voice kills this line.

### 0:28–0:29 — TITLE

> Full bleed. **GLUTT** logo. Sub-line, small: *your live AI chef*. Hold 0.8s. Cut.

### 0:29–0:35 — DEMO 1: ANY RECIPE

> **Shot:** Screen record. An Instagram Wellington reel playing → share sheet → Glutt → the recipe assembles itself: ingredients, steps, timings. Speed it up 2x if it's slow, that's fine.
>
> **Omar VO:** "The recipe came off Instagram. Glutt listens to the video and writes it down."
>
> *(14 words ≈ 6s)*
>
> **Text (small, lower third):** *no recipe was typed in*

### 0:35–0:43 — DEMO 2: HANDS-FREE

> **Shot A (5s):** Malik's hands buried in prosciutto and cling film, rolling the beef tight. Phone propped against the microwave, in frame, screen visible, Polly's pill glowing. **He never looks at it.**
>
> **Live audio, unscripted, real take:**
>
> Malik: "Polly, how tight should this be?"
> Polly: *(whatever she actually says)*
>
> **Shot B (2s):** Cut insert — ECU of the phone screen, Polly's caption line appearing, the listening pill.

> **Shot C (2s):** Back to hands. He keeps rolling without pausing.
>
> **Text (small, lower third, on Shot C):** *zero taps*

**Ask her something with a short answer.** "How tight should this be" beats "how do I make a Wellington." Short answers cut clean and sound confident.

### 0:43–0:53 — DEMO 3: SHE SEES THE PAN ← the aha, protect this

This is the beat the whole video exists for. Everything before it is setup.

> **Shot A (2s):** Pan of duxelles. Still visibly wet, steaming, glossy. Malik reaches for the handle to pull it off the heat.
>
> **Shot B (1s):** Quick cut to the phone, camera angled at the pan.
>
> **Live audio, real:** Polly stops him. Something in the shape of: *"That's still wet. If you wrap it now the pastry goes soggy. Give it a few more minutes."*
>
> **Shot C (3s):** Malik's hand freezes mid-air. Beat. He puts the pan back down on the heat. Looks off camera at Omar. Omar shrugs.
>
> **Shot D (3s):** Same pan, later, dry and dark, the moisture gone. Malik scrapes it out.
>
> **Text (over Shot C, holds):** *a soggy bottom is how a Wellington dies*

**The frozen hand is the shot.** Not the phone, not the UI. A human being physically stopped from making a mistake. If you get one clean take all day, make it this one.

**Why duxelles:** wet mushroom filling is *the* Wellington killer. It steams inside the pastry and turns the bottom to paste. Anyone who has ever attempted one knows this, and everyone else learns it in three seconds from your text card. It is the most legible catch available to you.

**Backup catches** if the duxelles beat doesn't land on the day:

- Beef not seared hard enough on one side → "that side's still gray, give it another minute"
- Wrapping while the beef is still warm → "let it cool or the pastry melts"
- Pulling it from the oven too early → ask her the pull temp

## Shot list

### Setup A — Locked wide (camera 1)

Tripod, eye level, sees the whole counter. This is your safety net and your continuity. **Roll it for the entire cook and never stop it.** Most of your best moments will be accidents you didn't know to point a camera at.

### Setup B — Handheld close (camera 2 / second phone)

Whoever isn't cooking runs this. Get in tight. Hands, pan, knife, faces. Handheld shake is fine here and actually helps the tone.

### Setup C — Phone-in-frame

The propped iPhone running Polly has to be *visible in frame* during both live demo beats. Not the hero, but present. Viewers need to understand that a voice is coming from a device on the counter that nobody is touching. Prop it against the microwave, angled so the screen catches light.

### Setup D — To-camera

Shoot after the Wellington goes in the oven. Kitchen behind you, some depth, warm light. Both of you have cooked for two hours by then, which is exactly the look you want. Do not shoot the to-camera pieces clean and fresh at the start.

### Setup E — Screen record

Separate, done on a laptop or quietly on the side. IG reel → share → recipe. Two takes, one slow, one already-scrolled. Also grab a clean screen record of the whole Polly session as fallback audio.

### B-roll shopping list

Cinematic Wellington shots that always work. Grab all of these, you'll use four.

- Duxelles hitting a screaming hot pan, steam wall
- Prosciutto laid out overlapping into a sheet
- The roll — cling film pulled tight, the log tightening
- Egg wash brushed on pastry, slow, close
- Scoring the pastry with the back of a knife, diagonal lines
- Flaky salt falling on the egg-washed top
- Oven door closing, warm light on faces
- Thermometer probe going in, the number
- The rest — steam coming off the pastry on the board
- **The slice.** Shoot it five times from three angles. This is your cold open, your payoff, and your thumbnail. Bring a sharp knife.

## Shoot day plan

A Wellington is roughly three hours. Shoot it chronologically once. Do not try to shoot out of order and reassemble a fake cook — it will look fake, and half of your usable footage will be things you didn't plan.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before | Prep the kitchen for camera: clear counters, kill overhead fluorescents if you can, one warm side light. Charge everything. Do a Polly test in the actual room, at cooking volume, with the extractor fan on. |
| Sear | Camera 1 rolling. Camera 2 tight on the pan. This is your first B-roll haul. |
| Duxelles | **The most important 20 minutes of the day.** Camera 1 wide, camera 2 on the pan, phone in frame. Let it genuinely be too wet, then genuinely ask her. Do not rehearse the line. |
| Wrap | The hands-free beat. Hands committed to the prosciutto before you say her name — the whole point is that you *can't* touch the phone. |
| Oven | Glasses beat, and any remaining pan B-roll. |
| Baking (~40 min) | Shoot every to-camera piece. Multiple takes, multiple phrasings. |
| Rest | Screen record the import flow. |
| Slice | Everything stops. Reset the room. Five takes, three angles. |

### Audio

- **To-camera:** lav mic, or a second phone recording in a shirt pocket. A kitchen at cooking volume will eat built-in mic audio.
- **Polly beats:** you want the *room* recording — her voice coming out of the phone speaker, in the space, with a fan running. That texture is the credibility. It sounds real because it is.
- **Belt and braces:** screen-record the Polly session simultaneously so you have a clean version of her audio if the room recording is unusable.
- Turn the extractor fan **down** during Polly beats, not off. Off looks staged. Roaring makes her unintelligible.

### Two things that will save you

1. **Don't blink on the money lines.** Specifically "while watching you do it" and "on the App Store." Say them flat, hold the lens.
2. **You will need more takes of Polly than you think.** Budget for it. Ask short questions. Ten short attempts beats two long ones.

## Edit plan

**Structure:** the video is a rubber band. It snaps out (the slice), pulls all the way back (neither of us could cook), then releases forward through the demo into the same slice, now earned. The viewer sees the payoff before they know what it cost. That's what makes them stay for the middle.

**Pacing:**

- 0:00–0:07 — slow. One cut. Let it breathe.
- 0:07–0:29 — medium. Two people talking, cut on the beat, no fat between sentences.
- 0:29–0:35 — fast. The import beat is the only place you let it get quick.
- 0:35–0:53 — **slow down again.** Instinct says speed up in the demo. Resist it. The frozen hand needs three full seconds to land. Jump-cutting the aha destroys the aha.
- 0:53–1:03 — resolve. Wide, calm, done.

**Sound design** does more work here than the edit does:

- Cold open: crust crackle, isolated, slightly boosted. Nothing else.
- Duxelles hitting the pan: sizzle, loud, on the cut.
- Polly's voice: do **not** EQ it into a clean voiceover. Leave it in the room.
- When she catches the mistake, **drop the music out entirely** for the length of her line. Bring it back on the frozen hand. That gap is the single most effective edit in the whole video.
- Knife scoring the pastry: crisp, close.

**Music:** one track, no vocals. Something warm with forward motion — a live-band feel, not a synth pad. It should enter at 0:07, drop out at 0:45 for Polly's catch, return at 0:49 on the frozen hand, and resolve on the end card. Do not use anything that sounds like SaaS explainer music.

**Text style:** one typeface, one weight, two sizes (big for the opener, small for lower thirds). White with a subtle shadow so it survives on pastry. Hard cuts only.

**Color:** warm, slightly lifted blacks. Do not crush it into a moody restaurant grade — this is a scrappy home kitchen and it should look like one. Match the app's palette if you can: cream and deep green.

**Aspect ratio:** shoot 4K horizontal, deliver 16:9 for the launch post. Then export a 9:16 version — the beat map survives a vertical crop almost unchanged, because everything important is in the center third. Shoot with that crop in mind.

## One honest note on the Meta glasses

You mentioned faking a glasses feature — Polly saying "you forgot to add this" through glasses that aren't coded yet. I'd push back on the specific version of that, and here's the distinction that matters:

**Polly looking through a camera at what's in the pan is a real, shipped feature.** It's in your App Store description. Demoing it through the phone is completely honest.

**Glasses are a form factor you haven't built.** If you stage a Polly line arriving through the glasses, you're not showing an early version of a real thing, you're showing a thing that doesn't exist. One reply of "wait, does that actually work?" on Twitter or LinkedIn and the whole launch becomes about that instead of about the product. Launch videos die from credibility hits, not from being unpolished.

**The version that gets you the same shot with none of the risk:**

- Shoot the duxelles catch with the phone. Real audio, real feature, real save.
- Wear the glasses anyway during the cook — they look great, they cost you nothing, and they signal where this is going without claiming anything.
- Give them three seconds at 0:51 with "Next, she just sees what you see" and an `in progress` label on screen.

You get the futuristic visual, you get to state the bet out loud, and nobody can accuse you of anything. And "next" is a stronger word than "now" here anyway — it tells investors and early users you know where you're going, which is exactly the point Eliam was making about betting on what people will have in five years.

## Alternate cold open (if the slice doesn't come out clean)

Wellingtons fail. If the center is gray or the bottom is soggy, you have a *better* video, not a worse one — but you have to commit to it.

Open on the failed slice. Text: `ATTEMPT ONE.` Cut to the two of you looking at it. Then run the same script, and let the payoff be attempt two, with Polly. The narrative gets stronger and the product claim gets much harder to argue with.

Decide this in the edit, not on the day. Just make sure you shoot attempt one either way.
